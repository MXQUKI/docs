Gears transfer rotation from one shaft to another. By pairing different tooth counts, you can trade output speed for torque:

* For more torque, drive a larger gear with a smaller one. Output speed decreases while torque increases.
* For more speed, drive a smaller gear with a larger one. Output speed increases while torque decreases.

Gear drives are especially useful when:

1. **Shafts are close together:** power needs to travel only a short distance.
2. **Space is limited:** a substantial speed reduction must fit in a compact mechanism.
3. **Direction must reverse:** the two shafts need to rotate in opposite directions.

## Gear Ratio

The ratio between two meshing gears determines output speed and torque. With equal tooth counts, the speed and torque remain the same in the ideal case.

### Speed Reduction: More Torque at Lower Speed

<DocImage src="/assets/docs/transmission/reduction_ratio.png" alt="20-tooth gear driving a 60-tooth gear" width={375} />

**Use this when:** the mechanism needs more torque.

**Arrangement:** a small driving gear turns a larger driven gear.

**Example:** a 20-tooth gear drives a 60-tooth gear.

* Driving-to-driven tooth ratio: 1:3
* Output speed is ⅓ of the input speed
* Output torque is 3 times the input torque, ignoring losses

### Speed Increase: Higher Speed at Lower Torque

<DocImage src="/assets/docs/transmission/accel_ratio.png" alt="40-tooth gear driving a 20-tooth gear" width={375} />

**Use this when:** the mechanism needs a higher maximum speed.

**Arrangement:** a large driving gear turns a smaller driven gear.

**Example:** a 40-tooth gear drives a 20-tooth gear.

* Driving-to-driven tooth ratio: 2:1
* Output speed is 2 times the input speed
* Output torque is ½ of the input torque, ignoring losses

## Rotation Direction

Gears also change the direction of rotation.

<DocImage src="/assets/docs/transmission/direction.png" alt="Rotation direction with two and three gears" width={375} />

* **Two gears:** the input and output rotate in opposite directions.
* **Three gears:** the input and output rotate in the same direction.

## Center Distance

Center distance is the distance between the two shaft centers:

<MathBlock expression={String.raw`C=\frac{D_1}{2}+\frac{D_2}{2}+a`} />

Here, C is center distance, D₁ and D₂ are the two pitch diameters, and a is the center-distance allowance. Use the same units throughout.

### Center-Distance Allowance

In practice, adding a small allowance (0.03mm–0.05mm) to the theoretical center distance can:

* Reduce gear wear
* Help the gears run smoothly
* Introduce slightly more backlash between the teeth

**Suggested allowances:**

* Close fit: +0.000mm (theoretical center distance, tightest mesh)
* Running clearance: +0.03mm to +0.05mm (recommended for smoother operation)

## Changing Ratios at a Fixed Center Distance

:::note

For gears of the same diametral pitch, keeping the total tooth count unchanged lets you change the ratio without moving the shafts.

:::
