## Center Distance

:::note

Because links can be added or removed, you can design a chain drive in this order:

1. Set the sprocket center distance to suit the robot layout.
2. Adjust the chain length for that distance.
3. Set the tension so the chain runs correctly on the sprockets.

:::

:::caution

* Avoid too little center distance: the sprockets may interfere with each other.
* Avoid excessive center distance: it can make chain tension difficult to control.

:::

## Chain Tension

A chain drive needs the right amount of tension to operate reliably.

### Why Does Tension Matter?

**Too loose:**

* The chain may skip teeth or come off the sprocket
* Wear increases
* Lost motion delays the mechanism's response

**Too tight:**

* Excessive loading on the chain, bearings, and shafts
* More friction consumes motor power
* Shorter component life

**Correctly tensioned:**

* Smooth, reliable power transmission
* Lower wear
* Better efficiency

<DocImage src="/assets/docs/transmission/tension.png" alt="Examples of chain tension" width={375} />

#### Ways to Adjust Tension

<Tabs>

<TabItem label="Adjust chain length">

Use a chain breaker and a master link to add or remove links.

**Use this when:** the chain is clearly too loose or too tight.

</TabItem>

<TabItem label="Adjust center distance">

Move a sprocket to change the distance between the two shaft centers.

**Use this when:** laying out the mechanism, or when the mounting arrangement allows adjustment.

**Tradeoff:** the mounting structure may need to be redesigned.

</TabItem>

<TabItem label="Tensioner (open-ended chain)">

<DocImage src="/assets/docs/transmission/tensioner_usage.png" alt="Tensioner in an open-ended chain assembly" width={375} />

* For chain arrangements that do not circulate in a complete loop
* Turn the center body to tension the chain

</TabItem>

<TabItem label="Idler sprocket (continuous loop)">

<DocImage src="/assets/docs/transmission/idle_gear.png" alt="Idler sprocket tensioning a continuous chain loop" width={375} />

* Add a small sprocket along the chain path
* Position it inside or outside the loop
* Increase sprocket engagement for more stable operation

:::note

An idler adjusts tension without changing the drive ratio.

:::

</TabItem>

</Tabs>

1. Unscrew both attachment bolts from the center body.
